## 1. Title

Introducing MiMo-V2.6 Series: Scaling RL and Open Agentic Models

## 2. Source

- Author / Organization: Xiaomi MiMo
- Link: https://mimo.xiaomi.com/mimo-v2-6
- Date: 2026-09-22

## 3. One-line Summary

- Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6 combines large-scale agentic reinforcement learning, open weights and training infrastructure, omnimodal capabilities, and aggressive API pricing to push the capability-cost frontier of open models.

## 4. Key Points

- MiMo-V2.6 ships as Pro and Flash omnimodal models, plus a Pro-UltraSpeed serving mode claiming up to 20× faster output at the same model quality.
- Xiaomi reports MiMo-V2.6-Pro scoring 46.32 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3, positioning it ahead of the open models compared in the release.
- The largest gains came from scaled reinforcement learning: each Pro and Flash run processed roughly 750K trajectories over 30 RL steps in under six days.
- Xiaomi reports RL compute costs of approximately $2.62M for Pro and $0.85M for Flash; these figures cover the RL stage, not the complete cost of developing the base models.
- DeepSWE v1.1 improved from roughly 58.4 → 72.6 for Pro and 48.8 → 65.7 for Flash during RL, suggesting substantial transfer to held-out long-horizon software-engineering tasks.
- Training used asynchronous large-batch RL, up to 1M-token context, 3.5–3.7B tokens per update, heterogeneous coding/agent/visual/cyber environments, and additional grader compute.
- Xiaomi exposed unusually detailed training telemetry through a live production RL dashboard, including failures and restarts, while releasing the technical report, environments, and RL code.
- The models extend agentic coding toward "Vibe World": generating games and 3D scenes, controlling simulated robots, building interfaces and presentations, composing music, and assisting research workflows.
- API pricing remains at V2.5 levels: Flash costs $0.14/$0.28 and Pro $0.435/$0.87 per million uncached input/output tokens, with much cheaper cache-hit input.
- Hacker News discussion focused less on raw benchmark leadership than on training transparency, cost efficiency, open weights, local/self-hosted deployment, and whether increasingly capable models are turning token generation into commodity infrastructure.

## 5. Deep Dive (Structured Understanding)

### Problem

Frontier model development faces several linked constraints:

1. Better pretrained models alone do not guarantee reliable long-horizon agent behavior.
2. Agentic RL becomes difficult when trajectories contain many tool calls and take substantial time to generate.
3. Training across coding, computer use, visual tasks, and other heterogeneous environments requires a scalable common infrastructure.
4. Increasing capability through brute-force compute is expensive, making intelligence-per-dollar increasingly important.
5. Benchmark results reveal little about how models were actually trained when training processes remain opaque.

### Approach

Xiaomi scaled reinforcement learning rather than treating post-training as a small final adjustment.

The system used:

- ~750K trajectories per model
- 30 RL updates
- 1,568 samples per update
- 3.5–3.7B tokens per step
- contexts up to 1M tokens
- fully asynchronous rollout/training infrastructure
- mixed coding, general-agent, visual, and cyber environments
- relative comparison and additional grader compute
- unified trajectory representations across agent frameworks
- controlled per-task sampling ratios
- frozen routing to reduce training drift
- adversarial evaluation, anomaly detection, verifier cross-checking, and reward-hacking defenses

A particularly important systems problem is **policy staleness**. Long agent rollouts may be generated by an older policy while training continues to update the current model. The public dashboard exposed metrics related to this train/inference divergence, making otherwise hidden RL engineering problems observable.

### Key Insight

The central idea is that **agentic capability can be improved by scaling verifiable feedback loops rather than relying only on larger pretrained models**.

For tasks where success can be checked—code execution, software tests, structured environments, simulations, or formal verification—the model can generate trajectories, receive reliable feedback, and iteratively shift probability toward successful behaviors.

This creates a practical self-improvement loop:

`model → trajectory → verifier/reward → RL update → improved model → new trajectory`

Xiaomi describes this direction as exploration of an "RSI path," but the demonstrated mechanism is scaled reinforcement learning over externally verifiable tasks rather than evidence of unrestricted recursive self-improvement.

### Result / Impact

The strongest evidence is the improvement during RL itself.

DeepSWE v1.1:

- Pro: ~58.4 → 72.6
- Flash: ~48.8 → 65.7

AutomationBench also improved steadily, while visual-task performance generally increased across the run.

The broader outcome is therefore not simply another model release. Xiaomi demonstrated a relatively transparent production-scale pipeline for turning verifiable environments, agent trajectories, graders, and compute into measurable capability gains.

The open release makes parts of that pipeline available for other researchers and infrastructure projects to inspect, reproduce, and modify.

## 6. Why It Matters

- **Post-training is becoming a major scaling axis.** Progress is increasingly driven not only by pretraining compute and parameter count but by RL environments, rollout generation, verifiers, and inference-time infrastructure.
- **Verifiability becomes strategically valuable.** Coding, formal mathematics, simulations, and structured computer tasks provide stronger reward signals than domains where output quality is subjective.
- **Model economics are shifting toward capability per dollar.** A model does not need to lead every benchmark if it delivers sufficient capability at dramatically lower inference cost.
- **Open weights affect infrastructure innovation.** Deployable models give projects such as vLLM, SGLang, quantization stacks, local inference systems, and independent hosting providers realistic workloads to optimize against.
- **Training transparency may become another competitive dimension.** Publishing failures, checkpoint metrics, costs, and RL telemetry provides information that conventional benchmark tables and model cards normally hide.
- **"Coding model" is becoming an outdated category.** Once code generation is combined with visual perception, computer use, simulation, and tools, coding becomes a mechanism for manipulating digital environments rather than merely producing source files.

## 7. Critical Analysis

- **The reported $0.85M/$2.62M costs are easy to misinterpret.** They describe the displayed RL runs, not the total expense of pretraining, data preparation, salaries, failed experiments, infrastructure, or earlier model development.
- **"Open-source" requires qualification.** Xiaomi releases weights and substantial RL infrastructure, but the available material does not provide complete transparency into all underlying training datasets. Open weights plus open post-training infrastructure is not equivalent to fully reproducible end-to-end training.
- **Benchmark breadth does not eliminate benchmark bias.** Several evaluations are Xiaomi's own in-house benchmarks, while external benchmarks still depend heavily on harnesses, prompts, inference settings, and task distributions.
- **Benchmark leadership is inconsistent.** MiMo-V2.6 is competitive on several coding and agent evaluations but remains substantially behind some proprietary models on benchmarks such as Terminal Bench 4.0, ExploitGym, and ExploitBench.
- **RL improvements sometimes coincide with increased token use.** Better benchmark scores therefore do not automatically imply better inference efficiency; capability, latency, trajectory length, and total cost must be evaluated separately.
- **Real-world reports are mixed.** HN users reported strong cost/performance and coding capability, but others observed overthinking, long completion times, architectural mistakes on ambiguous tasks, or provider/runtime-specific issues.
- **Data quality remains an underexplored variable.** The public dashboard reveals considerable training mechanics but comparatively little about dataset provenance, filtering, quality weighting, and expert curation.
- **The "RSI" framing is stronger than the demonstrated evidence.** The release shows iterative RL-driven capability improvement under engineered environments and verifiers; it does not demonstrate autonomous recursive improvement of the complete AI system.

## 8. Connections

### 1. On-policy RL and agent training

MiMo's approach connects to the broader move from static supervised demonstrations toward **on-policy reinforcement learning**, where models learn from trajectories resembling behavior their current policy can actually generate.

For long-running agents, this introduces systems problems such as stale rollout policies and train/inference divergence, turning distributed-systems engineering into part of the learning algorithm.

### 2. Verifiable rewards and coding agents

Software engineering is unusually suitable for scaled RL because tests, compilers, execution results, and repository state provide machine-checkable feedback.

The same principle extends to:

- Lean theorem proving
- computer-use environments
- simulations
- cybersecurity environments
- structured automation tasks

This helps explain why coding and tool-using agents have become central testbeds for post-training research.

### 3. Mixture-of-Experts and inference economics

MiMo-V2.6 uses large total parameter counts while activating only a fraction during inference. HN discussion cites approximately 309B total / 15B active parameters for Flash and 1.02T / 42B active for Pro.

This connects MiMo to the broader **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** trend: increasing model capacity without paying dense-model compute costs for every generated token.

### 4. Open weights → inference ecosystem

Open-weight releases create optimization targets for:

- vLLM
- SGLang
- quantization
- speculative decoding
- GPU memory offloading
- local inference hardware
- independent inference providers

The value of open models therefore extends beyond model access: they provide workloads around which an independent systems ecosystem can develop.

### 5. LLM commoditization and the Pareto frontier

The release emphasizes the **Pareto frontier** rather than absolute benchmark leadership.

A model lies on the capability-cost frontier when no alternative simultaneously provides greater capability and lower cost. This reframes competition from:

`Who has the smartest model?`

to:

`How much useful intelligence can be delivered per dollar, second, watt, and token?`

That shift favors efficient models for high-volume agent workloads even when a more expensive frontier model remains stronger on the hardest tasks.

### 6. From "vibe coding" to computer-use agents

"Vibe World" connects coding agents with computer-use agents, multimodal perception, Blender/game-engine automation, robotics simulation, and generative media.

The abstraction is moving from:

`natural language → code`

toward:

`natural language → agent → tools/code/environment → verified artifact`

The generated code becomes an intermediate implementation detail rather than the final product.

## 10. TL;DR

- Xiaomi scaled agentic RL across ~750K trajectories and 30 updates, producing large measured gains in coding and automation while exposing unusually detailed training telemetry.
- MiMo-V2.6 combines open weights, open RL infrastructure, multimodal agents, MoE efficiency, and low API pricing rather than competing solely on absolute benchmark leadership.
- The larger signal is a shift from pretraining-only scaling toward verifiable RL loops and from "best model" competition toward capability-per-cost infrastructure economics.
