# 1. Title

Canada's AI Strategy Shouldn't Include Secret Palantir Bills

---

# 2. Source

**Author / Organization:** Al Vigier / The Line (Substack)  
**Link:** https://www.readtheline.ca/  
**Date:** 2026-06-23

---

# 3. One-line Summary

Canada's AI strategy advocates sovereign AI, but its government continues relying on undisclosed contracts with foreign vendors like Palantir instead of openly procuring domestic AI solutions.

---

# 4. Key Points

- Canada's **AI for All** strategy positions the federal government as an "anchor customer" for domestic AI.
- Only about **12% of Canadian businesses** currently use AI, with a target of **60% adoption by 2034**.
- Canada's Department of National Defence quietly expanded a Palantir contract from **CAD 14.4M to approximately CAD 44.4M**, with around CAD 46.8M already spent.
- Ontario Provincial Police has used **Palantir Gotham** since 2015.
- Existing government AI procurement largely favors foreign vendors despite rhetoric around sovereign AI.
- The strategy allocates funding for equity investments, compute infrastructure, certification, and mission programs rather than committing to domestic procurement.
- Government equity investment may discourage private investors by increasing perceived political influence.
- Launching AI missions in healthcare risks slow adoption due to strict regulation and procurement complexity.
- The author argues procurement—not subsidies—is the strongest mechanism for building national AI capability.
- Greater transparency and procurement reform are necessary for credible sovereign AI policy.

---

# 5. Deep Dive (Structured Understanding)

## Problem

Canada promotes "sovereign AI" while government agencies already depend on foreign AI platforms, particularly Palantir, through procurement processes that receive little public disclosure.

Government support primarily consists of grants, investments, and certification programs rather than becoming an actual customer of Canadian AI companies.

## Approach

The author proposes shifting government policy toward procurement-driven industrial policy:

- Purchase Canadian AI products directly.
- Simplify procurement for smaller vendors.
- Require transparency in AI contracts.
- Allocate portions of departmental AI budgets specifically to Canadian-controlled products.

## Key Insight

Government purchasing power is more valuable than government ownership.

A startup gains long-term credibility through paying customers rather than government equity participation.

Procurement creates sustainable markets; subsidies create temporary support.

## Result / Impact

If procurement continues favoring foreign vendors:

- Domestic AI firms struggle to secure reference customers.
- Sovereign AI remains largely rhetorical.
- Public-sector dependence on foreign AI infrastructure increases.

A procurement-first strategy could accelerate domestic AI commercialization while strengthening national technological sovereignty.

# 6. Why It Matters

This article highlights a growing shift from **AI model competition** toward **AI sovereignty**.

Many governments now recognize that long-term competitiveness depends not only on developing AI models but also on controlling:

- procurement
- infrastructure
- data governance
- public-sector deployment

Government purchasing is emerging as an important industrial policy tool alongside traditional R&D funding.

---

# 7. Critical Analysis

- The author is CEO of Caseway, a Canadian AI company, creating a potential conflict of interest despite acknowledging it.
- The article assumes domestic procurement will naturally produce globally competitive AI companies without examining execution risks.
- It underestimates why governments often choose established vendors like Palantir, including security accreditation, operational maturity, and deployment history.
- The discussion focuses heavily on procurement while giving limited attention to technical capability, ecosystem maturity, or talent shortages.
- Healthcare is criticized as a poor starting point, but the article provides limited evidence that defense or public safety would achieve faster AI deployment.

# 8. Connections

### 1. Sovereign AI

Governments increasingly seek control over AI infrastructure, models, and strategic data instead of depending on foreign technology providers.

### 2. Government as Anchor Customer

Similar to historical government procurement supporting industries such as aerospace, semiconductors, and defense, AI procurement may become a major industrial policy instrument.

### 3. Palantir's Government Strategy

Palantir has built much of its competitive advantage through long-term defense, intelligence, and public-sector contracts rather than frontier AI model development.

### 4. European Digital Sovereignty

The article aligns with Europe's broader push toward digital sovereignty, trusted cloud infrastructure, and reduced dependence on U.S. technology vendors.

### 5. GovTech Procurement

The discussion reflects a wider challenge that government procurement processes often favor established incumbents over startups due to complex compliance requirements.

# 10. TL;DR

- Canada's AI strategy promotes sovereign AI while government procurement still relies heavily on foreign vendors like Palantir.
- The article argues that direct government procurement is more effective than subsidies or equity investments for building domestic AI ecosystems.
- Sovereign AI ultimately depends on procurement policy, infrastructure ownership, and transparent public-sector deployment—not just AI funding.
